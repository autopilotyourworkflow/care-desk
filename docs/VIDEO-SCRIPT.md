# Video script: the 2-minute walkthrough

Voice-over for a recorded walkthrough. About 290 words, roughly two minutes at a relaxed pace. It follows the 1-minute tour and adds a little on each screen. Cues in brackets are what is on screen; read only the plain lines.

## 1. A routine message (0:00)

[The welcome screen. Click **Start the 1-minute tour**. The desk opens on an order-status question with the drafted reply right under it. Open the **Facts come from** line under the draft to show the records behind the numbered facts.]

Hi, I'm Beam. This is Care Desk, a concept I built to show how a patient-support team could answer routine messages in seconds, without ever cutting corners on safety.

Here's a patient asking where their order is. Care Desk has already drafted a reply from their actual order record and the support policy, and every fact is numbered back to its source. The agent reads it, and sends it in one keystroke.

## 2. Open the trail (0:28)

[The details are open beside the message, with the check trail. Click **Show steps**, open **Personal details removed** to show what the AI saw, then open **Facts checked**.]

Beside it is the trail: everything that happened to this message, step by step. Names, phone numbers and addresses were swapped for placeholders before any AI saw it. A small, fast model sorted it, a stronger one wrote the reply, and then a checker matched every date, amount and tracking number against the records. If one didn't match, the draft would be blocked.

## 3. A reaction report stops (0:54)

[Next message: a patient reporting a reaction. The words are highlighted in the message, the reply says **A clinician answers first**, and the trail in the details (still open) stops at **Safety rules checked**.]

Now a patient describes a reaction after their dose. Plain safety rules run before the AI, and they stop right here. No AI reply at all. The patient's orders go on hold and a clinician is notified. The rules are tuned to over-escalate, because a false alarm costs a minute and a miss is not acceptable.

## 4. The clinician's view (1:16)

[Click **Open in the clinician view**. Point to the highlighted words, then **Orders on hold**.]

The clinician sees why it was escalated, with the exact words highlighted, the patient's context, and the hold. Releasing it needs a note.

## 5. The numbers (1:26)

[Test results headline, then the tricky cases. Then Insights and the assumptions panel.]

A labelled test set proves the routing, including deliberately tricky cases, like someone saying they're dying to get their order. It is re-run after every change, and a single missed safety case blocks the release. The team-lead view then estimates the time saved, using your own numbers.

## 6. Your turn (1:44)

[Try it. Type a short message and watch the live trail run.]

Finally, try your own message and watch it run live. Everything here is fictional, and a person always sends. Thanks for taking a look.

[End on the desk. About 2:00.]
